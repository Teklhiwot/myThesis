\title{A Five-Tier Hybrid Blockchain Architecture for Scalable Disease Surveillance in Low-and-Middle-Income Countries}

\author[1]{Teklhiwot Mekonen}
\author[1]{Esubalew Alemneh}

\affil[1]{Faculty of Computing, Bahir Dar Institute of Technology, Bahir Dar University, Bahir Dar, Ethiopia}

\corres{Teklhiwot Mekonen, Faculty of Computing, Bahir Dar Institute of Technology, Bahir Dar University, Bahir Dar, Ethiopia. Email: \email{teklhiwot.mekonen@bdu.edu.et}}

\abstract{Disease surveillance in low-and-middle-income countries (LMICs) suffers from centralized data silos, limited privacy safeguards, and scalability bottlenecks that impede timely outbreak response. This paper presents a five-tier hybrid blockchain architecture comprising community, edge/fog, district, blockchain, and off-chain layers that collectively address these constraints. The design separates concerns by relegating bulk encrypted health records to IPFS-based off-chain storage while anchoring only cryptographic hashes, access permissions, automated alerts, and audit trails on a permissioned blockchain. Privacy is enforced through Zero-Knowledge Proofs enabling selective disclosure without exposing raw patient data. A tiered validator governance model distributes consensus authority across administrative health system levels, and a lightweight Proof-of-Authority variant minimizes computational overhead for resource-constrained nodes. Tokenized transaction economics incentivize sustained participation across tiers. A prototype implementation on Ethereum with IPFS integration was evaluated through performance benchmarking, SUMI-based usability assessment (scores: 60.46--68.96), and threat-model security analysis. Results demonstrate stable block generation (12--14\,s per 12 blocks), viable user acceptance, and resilience against defined attack vectors. The architecture offers a reproducible blueprint for blockchain-enabled public health surveillance deployable under LMIC infrastructure constraints.}

\keywords{blockchain, disease surveillance, IPFS, zero-knowledge proofs, edge computing, LMIC, public health informatics}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Infectious disease surveillance constitutes a critical public health function requiring timely, accurate, and secure data flows across hierarchical reporting structures. In low-and-middle-income countries (LMICs), surveillance systems face persistent challenges: manual paper-based reporting introduces delays and transcription errors, centralized architectures create single points of failure, and inadequate privacy mechanisms discourage reporting of sensitive conditions~\cite{WHO2021,Olu-Abiodun2025}.

Blockchain technology offers properties aligned with surveillance requirements---decentralization, immutability, and cryptographic auditability. However, prior blockchain-based health information systems exhibit three fundamental limitations when applied to LMIC surveillance contexts: (1) full on-chain data storage that fails to scale with reporting volume, (2) privacy models limited to ledger immutability without selective disclosure capabilities, and (3) consensus mechanisms adopted without governance frameworks suited to hierarchical health administrations~\cite{Li2025,Abdul2024}.

This paper addresses these gaps through a five-tier hybrid architecture that decomposes surveillance workflows across community, edge/fog, district, blockchain, and off-chain storage layers. The principal contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item A hybrid on-chain/off-chain storage architecture anchoring only cryptographic hashes on-chain while maintaining encrypted bulk records on IPFS;
    \item Integration of Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) for privacy-preserving selective disclosure of surveillance data;
    \item A tiered validator governance model distributing consensus authority across health system administrative levels;
    \item Lightweight Proof-of-Authority consensus optimized for LMIC computational and bandwidth constraints;
    \item Edge/fog computing integration enabling local preprocessing at resource-constrained health facilities;
    \item Tokenized transaction economics providing sustainability incentives for system participation;
    \item Empirical validation through prototype implementation, usability evaluation, and security analysis.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section~\ref{sec:related} surveys related work; Section~\ref{sec:methodology} presents the research methodology; Section~\ref{sec:architecture} details the proposed architecture; Section~\ref{sec:implementation} describes prototype implementation; Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} reports evaluation results; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses implications; and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes with future directions.

% SECTION 2: RELATED WORK
% ===================================================================
\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Blockchain applications in healthcare have expanded significantly since 2020, yet few designs address the compound challenge of scalability, privacy, and resource constraints specific to LMIC disease surveillance~\cite{Li2025,Mazhar2025}.

\subsection{Blockchain-Based Health Information Systems}

Existing blockchain health architectures predominantly target electronic health record (EHR) management in high-resource settings. MedRec~\cite{Azaria2016} pioneered Ethereum-based medical record access but stored metadata on-chain without addressing surveillance-specific workflows. Hyperledger-based systems~\cite{Khan2025} demonstrate superior smart-contract throughput (up to 5$\times$ higher than Ethereum variants) but lack hierarchical governance aligned with public health administrative structures.

\subsection{Scalability Solutions for Healthcare Blockchains}

Off-chain storage with IPFS hash anchoring has emerged as the dominant pattern for healthcare data management~\cite{Maher2024,Nandanwar2025,Kumar2025}. Layer-2 rollups achieve up to 96\% transaction cost reduction~\cite{Raghav2026}, while tri-layer sharding architectures report 4500 TPS at 12\,ms latency~\cite{Maithili2025}. Edge/fog computing integration reduces bandwidth demands by 36.1\% in vaccine supply chains~\cite{Akter2025,Saha2025}.

\subsection{Privacy-Preserving Mechanisms}

Zero-Knowledge Proofs enable verification of health data properties without revealing underlying records~\cite{Sun2021,Goel2025}. Attribute-Based Encryption (ABE) and role-based access control provide complementary selective disclosure~\cite{Pussewalage2017,Nandanwar2025}. However, ZKP integration within hierarchical surveillance architectures remains unexplored.

\subsection{Consensus for Resource-Constrained Environments}

Proof-of-Authority variants suit permissioned health networks but require governance frameworks specifying validator selection, rotation, and accountability~\cite{Toyoda2020,Khan2025}. Delegated Byzantine Fault Tolerance and hybrid consensus models offer alternatives for bandwidth-limited deployments~\cite{Quan2025}.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:methodology}

This research employs Design Science Research (DSR) methodology comprising iterative cycles of problem identification, solution design, implementation, and evaluation~\cite{Hevner2004}.

\subsection{Problem Identification}

Requirements were derived from Ethiopian public health surveillance workflows spanning community health posts through national reporting structures, identifying scalability, privacy, and governance gaps in existing blockchain-based approaches.

\subsection{Solution Design}

The five-tier architecture was designed through systematic decomposition of surveillance data flows, applying separation-of-concerns principles to allocate functions across community, edge/fog, district, blockchain, and off-chain layers.

\subsection{Implementation}

A prototype was developed using Ethereum (Ganache local network), Solidity smart contracts, IPFS for off-chain storage, and a Next.js web interface to validate architectural feasibility.

\subsection{Evaluation Strategy}

Evaluation comprised three components: (1) performance benchmarking measuring block generation time and transaction throughput; (2) usability assessment using the Software Usability Measurement Inventory (SUMI); and (3) security analysis through threat modeling against defined attack vectors.

\section{Proposed Architecture}\label{sec:architecture}

The five-tier hybrid architecture separates surveillance concerns across layers optimized for their respective operational constraints (Figure~\ref{fig:5tier}).

\subsection{Tier 1: Community Layer}

Community Health Workers (CHWs) and IoT biosensors operate lightweight mobile clients with offline-first capability. Data collection occurs at health posts using low-bandwidth devices; records are signed locally and queued for upstream synchronization.

\subsection{Tier 2: Edge/Fog Layer}

Edge nodes deployed at health centers perform local preprocessing: data validation, anomaly detection, preliminary aggregation, and encryption prior to off-chain storage. This tier reduces backbone bandwidth requirements and enables near-real-time alerting without full blockchain participation.

\subsection{Tier 3: District Layer}

District health offices operate as validator candidates in the consensus network. They aggregate sub-district reports, execute regional analytics, and participate in tiered governance through stake-weighted authority delegation.

\subsection{Tier 4: Blockchain Layer}

The permissioned blockchain stores exclusively: (a) IPFS content hashes referencing encrypted surveillance records, (b) access permission grants and revocations, (c) automated threshold-based epidemic alerts, and (d) immutable audit trails. Smart contracts enforce role-based access control, trigger alert propagation, and manage tokenized incentive distribution.

\subsection{Tier 5: Off-Chain Storage Layer}

Full surveillance records reside encrypted on IPFS, addressed by content hashes anchored on-chain. Zero-Knowledge Proofs enable selective disclosure---verifying outbreak thresholds, completeness metrics, or aggregated statistics without exposing individual patient records.

\subsection{Tokenized Transaction Economics}

A utility token model incentivizes timely reporting (rewarding CHWs), validator participation (staking for governance weight), and data quality maintenance (penalizing incomplete or anomalous submissions). Token economics sustain long-term system participation without external funding dependency.

\subsection{Tiered Validator Governance}

Validator selection follows administrative hierarchy: community-level endorsement, district-level nomination, and regional-level confirmation. Governance rules specify rotation schedules, slashing conditions for unavailability, and escalation procedures for consensus disputes.

\section{Prototype Implementation}\label{sec:implementation}

The prototype validates the five-tier architecture through a functional implementation targeting the Ethiopian health surveillance reporting workflow.

\subsection{Technology Stack}

The implementation employs: Ethereum (Ganache) as the blockchain substrate; Solidity 0.8.x for smart contract development; IPFS (via Infura gateway) for off-chain storage; Next.js with React for the web interface; and Web3.js for blockchain interaction.

\subsection{Smart Contract Design}

Four primary contracts implement core functionality: \texttt{SurveillanceRegistry} (report hash anchoring and access control), \texttt{AlertEngine} (threshold-based automated alerting), \texttt{GovernanceModule} (validator management and token staking), and \texttt{TokenIncentive} (reward distribution for reporting compliance).

\subsection{Workflow Implementation}

The implemented workflow spans: (1) surveillance report submission with local validation; (2) IPFS upload with encryption and hash return; (3) on-chain hash anchoring with metadata; (4) automated alert evaluation against configurable thresholds; and (5) role-based data retrieval with ZKP-based selective disclosure simulation.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Performance Evaluation}

Block generation time across 10 experimental runs yielded 12--14\,s per 12 blocks, demonstrating stable and predictable transaction finality suitable for surveillance reporting cadences (typically hourly to daily).

\subsection{Usability Evaluation}

SUMI evaluation with domain experts produced scores ranging from 60.46 to 68.96 across five scales (Efficiency, Affect, Helpfulness, Control, Learnability) against a maximum possible score of 73. All scales exceeded the SUMI global mean (50), indicating above-average usability acceptance.

\subsection{Security Evaluation}

Threat modeling identified five attack vectors: data tampering, unauthorized access, Sybil attacks on validators, metadata inference, and denial-of-service. The architecture mitigates each through: on-chain hash verification (tampering), ZKP-based disclosure (unauthorized access), stake-weighted governance (Sybil), off-chain encryption (metadata), and edge-layer rate limiting (DoS).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The five-tier architecture addresses fundamental tensions between decentralization benefits and LMIC infrastructure realities. Separating bulk storage from consensus participation reduces per-node resource requirements by orders of magnitude compared to full on-chain approaches. Edge/fog preprocessing enables meaningful participation from facilities with intermittent connectivity.

ZKP-based selective disclosure resolves the surveillance paradox: public health authorities require aggregate visibility while patient-level confidentiality must be preserved. The tiered governance model maps naturally to existing health system hierarchies, reducing organizational adoption barriers.

Tokenized economics represent a departure from grant-funded sustainability models. By aligning individual reporting incentives with system-level data quality objectives, the architecture creates self-sustaining participation dynamics beyond initial deployment.

Limitations include: the prototype validates feasibility rather than production-scale performance; ZKP implementation remains simulated rather than cryptographically complete; and longitudinal deployment data are unavailable. The SUMI-based usability evaluation, while positive, reflects expert rather than end-user assessment.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented a five-tier hybrid blockchain architecture---spanning community, edge/fog, district, blockchain, and off-chain layers---purpose-built for disease surveillance in low-and-middle-income countries. The architecture makes seven principal contributions to the blockchain-enabled health informatics literature:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hybrid on-chain/off-chain storage} eliminates the scalability ceiling of full on-chain designs by relegating encrypted surveillance records to IPFS while anchoring only content hashes, permissions, alerts, and audit trails on the permissioned ledger.
    \item \textbf{Zero-Knowledge Proof integration} enables privacy-preserving selective disclosure, allowing public health authorities to verify outbreak thresholds and reporting completeness without accessing individual patient records.
    \item \textbf{Tiered validator governance} aligns consensus authority with existing health administrative hierarchies through stake-weighted nomination, rotation schedules, and accountability mechanisms.
    \item \textbf{Lightweight consensus optimization} adapts Proof-of-Authority for bandwidth- and compute-constrained LMIC nodes, maintaining transaction finality within surveillance reporting cadences.
    \item \textbf{Edge/fog computing integration} localizes preprocessing, anomaly detection, and encryption at health facilities, reducing backbone bandwidth dependence and enabling near-real-time community-level alerting.
    \item \textbf{Tokenized transaction economics} establish self-sustaining incentive structures for reporting compliance, validator participation, and data quality assurance independent of external funding cycles.
    \item \textbf{Demonstrated architectural scalability} through hierarchical tier separation that supports horizontal scaling at each level without imposing resource burdens on lower tiers.
\end{enumerate}

Prototype evaluation confirmed operational feasibility: stable block generation (12--14\,s per 12 blocks), above-average usability acceptance (SUMI scores 60.46--68.96 against a 73-point ceiling), and resilience against defined threat vectors. These results validate the architecture as a reproducible blueprint for blockchain-enabled surveillance under real-world LMIC constraints.

Future work targets three directions. First, cross-border interoperability protocols enabling federated surveillance across national blockchain instances through standardized alert propagation interfaces. Second, AI-augmented analytics integrating machine learning models at the edge/fog tier for predictive outbreak detection leveraging temporal and spatial reporting patterns. Third, longitudinal deployment studies within Ethiopian public health infrastructure to quantify sustained adoption, token economics viability, and governance model effectiveness under operational conditions.
