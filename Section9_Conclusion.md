# 9. Conclusion and Future Work

This paper presented a blockchain-based architectural model for disease early warning and surveillance (DEWS), implemented as a functional prototype on the Ethereum platform. The model integrates four core surveillance processes—hierarchical disease case reporting, laboratory result reporting, automated report completeness verification, and multi-dimensional epidemiological data analysis—within a decentralized, immutable, and traceable smart contract architecture. By encoding surveillance business logic directly into smart contracts, the model eliminates reliance on centralized intermediaries and provides cryptographically verifiable audit trails across all reporting tiers.

A three-dimensional evaluation validated the model against operational requirements. Performance evaluation demonstrated stable block generation times of 12–14 seconds under Proof of Authority consensus, satisfying the temporal constraints of both immediate and periodic surveillance reporting workflows. Usability evaluation via the standardized SUMI instrument (n = 26 domain professionals) yielded scores of 60.46–68.96 across all six scales, significantly exceeding the normative population mean of 50 and confirming practical acceptability among epidemiologists, public health workers, and laboratory technicians. Security evaluation through structured threat modeling demonstrated inherent resistance to man-in-the-middle, denial-of-service, and traffic analysis attacks via blockchain's native cryptographic identity management, tokenized transaction economics, and distributed data replication.

Collectively, the proposed model addresses the principal limitations of existing disease surveillance systems—data centralization, security vulnerabilities, traceability gaps, manual completeness verification, and inter-organizational interoperability barriers—through the systematic application of distributed ledger technology to the public health domain. The work contributes a novel integration of blockchain architecture with the complete DEWS workflow, advancing beyond prior studies that addressed either health data storage or predictive analytics in isolation.

**Future work.** Several directions merit further investigation: (1) extension of the model to encompass public health emergency response and recovery processes downstream of surveillance; (2) integration of machine learning modules with the blockchain architecture for predictive outbreak detection capabilities; (3) migration from test network deployment to a production-grade operational system with empirical scalability assessment under concurrent multi-region reporting loads; (4) evaluation of alternative consensus mechanisms and blockchain platforms to optimize throughput and cost for resource-constrained health systems; and (5) comparative evaluation against similar blockchain-based health information systems using standardized benchmarking frameworks.
